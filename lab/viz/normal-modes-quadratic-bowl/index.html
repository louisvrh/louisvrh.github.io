The matrix K

The point h, in both coordinate systems

What to notice

  • Drag h around a contour. Kh is always perpendicular to the contour (it is ∇V), so it lines up with h only where the contour is perpendicular to the radius: at the ends of the ellipse's axes. Those are the eigenvectors.
  • On an eigenvector the force −Kh = −λh points straight back at the origin, so released from rest the point oscillates along that line at : a normal mode. Anywhere else the force has a sideways part and the two directions mix.
  • In the rotated coordinates there is no cross term, so : two independent 1D springs. The steep axis (large λ) is the fast mode and the shallow axis is the slow mode.
Model and assumptions
  • K is real symmetric, so its eigenvalues are real and its eigenvectors orthogonal. They are found in closed form: , with e1 at angle . Ordering: λ1 ≥ λ2.
  • "Parallel" is flagged when the angle between h and Kh is within 2° of 0° or 180°; dragging within 1.2° of an eigenvector snaps onto it.
  • Equal masses (M = mI) are assumed throughout, so the normal modes are the eigenvectors of K itself. With unequal masses you would solve instead.
  • Plot domain: |h|, |k| ≤ 2.2 (contours), disc |h| ≤ 2 (surface).

The motion is two independent modes

What to notice

  • Because K is already diagonal, x and y are the normal coordinates: the 2D path is just two 1D oscillations played at once, and the dots on the axes never interact.
  • Ex and Ey are separately constant, so the path is trapped in the rectangle |x| ≤ X, |y| ≤ Y, a tighter constraint than energy alone (the ellipse V = E).
  • If ω2/ω1 is rational the curve closes. If it is irrational, like √2, it never repeats and eventually fills the rectangle.

Masses vs modes

What to notice

  • In-phase starts on the a-axis and stays there (b = 0): the middle spring is never stretched, so ωa = √(κ/m). Anti-phase stays on the b-axis, with ωb = √((κ+2κc)/m). Each is a straight-line motion through the bowl along an eigenvector.
  • Pull one aside: x1(t) and x2(t) are tangled, and energy sloshes from mass 1 to mass 2 and back. But a(t) and b(t) are clean sinusoids and Ea, Eb (dashed) are constant. The modes never exchange energy; only the masses do.
  • Try weak coupling: ωa ≈ ωb, so x1 = A cos(½(ωb−ωa)t) cos(½(ωa+ωb)t) shows clean beats, and all the energy moves to mass 2 after t = π/(ωb−ωa).
Model and assumptions
  • Wall–κ–m–κc–m–κ–wall. Your notes' case of three identical springs is κc = κ (the default). The extra κc slider is there so you can weaken the coupling and see slow beats.
  • Arbitrary units, with defaults m = κ = 1. Small oscillations, no damping, no gravity. Spring displacements in the drawing are exaggerated (1 unit is 10% of the wall-to-wall distance).
  • Exact normal-mode solution evaluated at each frame, with no integrator and no drift. The core was checked against velocity Verlet with dt = 10⁻³: the two agree to 3×10⁻⁴ after 20 periods. E1 + E2 = Ea + Eb holds to 10⁻¹⁵.
