Circular contour ⇒ uniform elastic sheet

At small |k| the contours are circles: ω = ωDa|k| whatever the direction. Many atoms fit in one wavelength, so the wave averages over the grid and cannot tell [10] from [11]; the crystal behaves like a continuous elastic sheet with one sound speed.

Squarish contour ⇒ the wave feels the grid

As |k|a approaches π the contours turn into rounded squares, then the diamond ω = 2ωD. At fixed wavelength ω now depends on direction: along [10] (ω = 2ωD at the zone edge) vs along [11] (ω = 2√2 ωD at the zone corner), so one wave outruns the other.

Energy flows normal to the contours

vg = ∇kω points across contours. For circles that is along k; for squarish contours it can point at an angle to k, so a wave packet drifts sideways of its wave vector. At the zone edge and corner vg = 0: standing waves.

What to notice

  • At |k|a = 0.2 the frequency ratio ω([11])/ω([10]) is 1.0008 (spread under 0.1%); at |k|a = 2 it is 1.092. Press "Long wavelength" then "Short wavelength" and watch the race lanes: dots keep pace, then separate.
  • Along [10] the rows of atoms are a apart and each spring lies along the wave; along [11] successive rows are a/√2 apart and every spring is off-axis. At long wavelength this is invisible, at wavelength of a few a the wave samples it.
  • Drag the point at large |k|: the red vg arrow (normal to the contour) leaves the direction of k, e.g. about 25° off at |k|a = 2.5, θ = 22.5°. At (π, π) it vanishes.
Model and assumptions

Scalar model: each atom has one displacement un, nearest-neighbour springs along x and y only (no diagonal springs, no second polarisation); this is the kz = 0 section of the simple-cubic result and gives Eq. 4.12. ωD = √(κ/m) is the spring frequency (course convention). Units a = ωD = 1; the displayed amplitude is fixed and arbitrary (linear model).

The animation is the exact analytic plane-wave solution uij(t) = cos(kxi + kyj − ωkt), not a numerical integration, so there is no drift. The 16×16 block is a window of an infinite lattice; no boundary conditions are imposed. Tests (Node): residual of the equation of motion 2×10⁻¹⁴ (analytic utt) and 5×10⁻⁶ (finite-difference utt); an independent periodic 16×16 velocity-Verlet lattice conserves energy to 6×10⁻¹¹ over 25 periods and tracks the analytic mode to its expected phase error.

Small-|k| expansion: sin²(x/2) ≈ x²/4 − x⁴/48 gives (ω/ωDa|k|)² ≈ 1 − (ka)²(cos⁴θ + sin⁴θ)/12. The leading term is isotropic; direction first enters at order (ka)², so ω([11])/ω([10]) ≈ 1 + (ka)²/48. The speed shown for wavefronts is the phase speed ω/|k|. Beyond the zone edge a wave is indistinguishable from its Bragg-folded image, so |k| is limited so that k stays inside the first Brillouin zone: π/a along [10], π√2/a along [11].
