What to notice

  • Pluck one atom: the pulse splits into two packets that run apart at up to v = ωDa (dashed lines), while short-wavelength ripples lag behind. Different k travel at different speeds, which is dispersion.
  • Zone-edge mode (−1)n: the shape never moves, every spring is stretched or squeezed by 2u, and it oscillates at ω = 2ωD. The uniform shift (k = 0) has no force at all.
  • Hover an atom: net force is the sum of its right-spring and left-spring forces. A stretched right spring (un+1 > un) pulls it right.
Model and assumptions

Harmonic (nearest-neighbour Hooke's law) chain of N identical atoms, longitudinal motion only, κ and m constant, a = m = κ = 1. Equation integrated: ün = un+1 − 2un + un−1.

Integrator: velocity Verlet (symplectic), fixed dt = 0.01 in units of 1/ωD, accumulator loop independent of frame rate. Energy oscillates at the 10−5 level with no secular drift. Damping, if switched on, is applied as exact exponential half-kicks and is not part of the lecture model.

Boundary conditions: fixed = both ends attached to rigid walls by springs (u0 = uN+1 = 0, N + 1 springs); free = end atoms have one spring only; periodic = un+N = un (Lecture 5), drawn as a ring where u is tangential. The mode plot uses the exact normal modes of each case: sine modes (fixed), cosine modes (free), Fourier modes (periodic); with fixed or free ends k is the standing-wave value πj/(N+1) or πj/N. A uniform shift is a zero-force mode only for free and periodic ends.

Editing: dragging pauses the run and makes the current positions the new starting condition (velocities zeroed in displacement mode; kept in kick mode). Large displayed magnification can make atoms overlap on screen; the physics is unaffected (the equation is linear).
