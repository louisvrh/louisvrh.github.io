Acoustic

At long wavelength every cell moves together and the two atoms inside each cell move together: no spring is stretched, so the restoring force vanishes and ω → 0 (ω ≈ v|k|). Atoms in a cell are in step for all k (same direction, apart from the small lag across a/2).

Optical

The two atoms beat against each other in every cell, with the centre of mass fixed at k = 0. The intra-cell spring is stretched even though every cell does the same thing, so ω stays finite: ω2 = 2C/μ. Amplitude ratio A1/A2 = −m2/m1.

What is and is not true

“Opposite motion inside a cell” is a property of the optical branch only. For k ≠ 0 the relative displacement u(2)n − u(1)n carries a factor eikna, so it changes from cell to cell; it is the same in every cell only at k = 0.

What to notice
  • Slide k → 0: the acoustic row freezes into a rigid shift of the whole chain (atoms together, ω → 0, springs unstretched), while the optical row keeps oscillating with the atoms opposite and the cell centre of mass at rest.
  • Move k to π/a: the acoustic mode has only the heavy atoms moving, the optical mode only the light atoms. Raise m1/m2 to open the gap between them; at m1=m2 it closes.
  • At intermediate k, look at the dashed cell and its neighbours: the ratio of the two atoms' amplitudes and the cell-to-cell phase ka both matter.
Model and assumptions
  • Infinite 1D chain of alternating masses m1 (atom 1) and m2 (atom 2), nearest-neighbour harmonic springs, longitudinal motion (transverse motion obeys the same equations). Atom 1 sits at x = na, atom 2 at x = na + a/2, whatever the spring constants. The animation shows a 12-cell (8 on narrow screens) window of the infinite chain, so k is a free continuous parameter.
  • Units: m2 = 1 and C = 1; ω is in units of √(C/m2), time in √(m2/C). The sliders set m1/m2, C1/C and C2/C.
  • Each mode is the exact normal-mode solution u(j)n = Re[Ajei(kna−ωt)], with ω2 from m1m2ω4 − (C1+C2)(m1+m2)ω2 + 4C1C2sin2(ka/2) = 0 and (A1, A2) from the 2×2 eigenproblem, normalised to max(|A1|,|A2|) = 1. No numerical integration is needed; the animation is the analytic solution. Overall phase of A is arbitrary.
  • Both rows share one clock, so relative frequencies are true. As k → 0 the acoustic row slows to a stop because ω → 0; tick “each row at its own pace” to see the pattern.
  • For C1 ≠ C2 the amplitude ratio B1/B2 is complex in general (the plot also shows its imaginary part, dashed), and at the zone edge both atoms move in both branches. Equal springs give the clean “one sublattice at rest” result. Arrow lengths are displacement ×2.5.
  • Checked offline against the equations of motion on an explicit chain, a full 2N×2N dynamical-matrix diagonalisation, and a velocity-Verlet simulation of a periodic chain (energy conserved, positions match the animated formula).
