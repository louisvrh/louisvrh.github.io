What to notice

  • N = 1: the scattered intensity is a perfect circle, so every kout is allowed and q is unconstrained. Raise N and the circle collapses into discrete spikes of width about 2π/(Na).
  • Beams exist only where a reciprocal-lattice point sits on the Ewald circle (2kin·G + G² = 0). Rotate kin or change |kin| and beams switch on and off as the circle sweeps through points; for large N use the snap buttons or the Fig 3.6 preset to land exactly on them.
  • For |kin| < π/a (λ > 2a) the circle is too small to reach any G ≠ 0, so only the forward beam survives. The first Bragg beam appears at λ = 2a (back-scattering, 2θ = 180°).
  • Switch on the overlay: place kin, then G at its tip. The arrow tip lands on the tip of kout only for beams that satisfy 2kin·G + G² = 0, and the Bragg planes drawn normal to G make equal angles θ with kin and kout.
  • With the overlay on, the ringed atoms are one Bragg plane of the active beam (step m with ◀ ▶ or click inside the crystal). They all scatter in phase for specular reflection, and neighbouring planes then add in phase when 2d sinθ = Nλ. Switch on “colour all planes” to see every atom belongs to exactly one plane of the family.
  • Structure selector: semicrystalline (a fraction p ordered) keeps the Bragg peaks at the same angles with intensity p², on top of a diffuse background from the random atoms and the vacancies they leave; fully amorphous gives only a broad halo near |q| ≈ 2π/(nearest-neighbour distance), roughly 6 to 9 per a here; a basis atom at τ = (½,½)a with f2 = f1 cancels (1,0)-type beams exactly and quadruples (1,1)-type ones.
Model and assumptions

Point-like identical atoms (form factor constant) on an N×N square lattice, lattice constant a = 1; single scattering from each atom (kinematic, no absorption or multiple scattering); plane-wave incident beam; far-field (Fraunhofer) detection; 2D scattering plane. The intensity is the exact closed form of the double sum, I/N⁴ = [sin(Nqxa/2)/(N sin(qxa/2))]² [sin(Nqya/2)/(N sin(qya/2))]², with q = kout − kin and |kout| = |kin| (no time stepping is involved). The core was checked against the brute-force sum over all N² atoms.

The reciprocal-space circle is the locus of allowed q = kout − kin; its brightness shows I. Beams in the table are lattice points within the finite-size peak (|δq| < 2π/(Na) per axis) of the circle, with peak intensity relative to the exact-Bragg value. At exact Bragg conditions for G = n(h₁’,h₂’) the table gives the plane spacing d = a/√(h₁’²+h₂’²) and 2d sinθ = nλ. Fig 3.6 gives 8 points on the circle: 7 deflected beams plus the undeflected forward beam (G = 0).

G-triangle overlay: wavevectors are not real-space displacements. They are drawn from the crystal centre with their true directions and one shared length scale (px per 2π/a, printed on the panel, set so that |kin| fills about 60% of the panel radius). Bragg planes are the lines (p1,p2)·r = integer (with the half-integer offset for even N) where (p1,p2) = (h1,h2)/n, spacing d = a/√(p1²+p2²) = 2π/|g|, drawn only while d is at least 3 px. For a detuned beam (peak within the finite-size width but not exactly on the circle) kout keeps length |kin| in the direction of kin + G, and the leftover gap is drawn dotted.

Structure modes: intensity is the direct sum I(q) = |Σj fj e-iq·rj|² over the actual atom positions, on a 1/6° angular grid, normalised by (Σf)² for the plot and by Ns² (Ns = N² lattice sites) for the readouts. The perfect crystal still uses the closed form (identical to the direct sum to 1e-12). N is capped at 30 in the other modes. Semicrystalline: a fixed random subset of round(pNs) sites stay on the lattice; the others move to uniform random positions inside the N×N area (seeded, Reshuffle changes the seed). The expected diffuse level is (1−p²)Ns, because the vacancies also scatter diffusely, not just (1−p)Ns. Amorphous: uniform random start, then overlapping pairs are pushed apart until all separations are at least 0.8a (same density as the lattice, so jammed-fluid-like); it has excluded-volume short-range order only. Thermal: Gaussian displacement σ per axis, giving e−q²σ² on the coherent peak. Basis: second atom at R + τ with relative strength f2/f1. The G-plane overlay uses only the lattice-site atoms (ordered ones in the semicrystalline mode) and is hidden for the amorphous structure. Background is the mean intensity over directions more than 4 peak widths from any beam.

Peak (Laue) widths shrink as 1/N, so for N = 40 a tiny detuning from the Bragg condition kills the beam, which is why a real crystal is rotated or a range of wavelengths is used.
