Lattice waves vs phonons: one vibrational state, two pictures

Left: atoms of a ring displaced by a superposition of travelling waves. Right: the same state as a gas of phonons, nk quanta of energy ħωk in each mode k. Heat the crystal and watch quanta (and wave amplitude) appear. (In 1D the rms displacement grows with T and N, so lower the magnification G at high T.)

Mapping the two pictures. A normal mode is a point k on the dispersion; its wave amplitude grows with how many phonons sit in it (Ak ∝ √((nk+½)/ωk)), so the left motion is built from the right-hand occupations. Energy comes in lumps ħωk: heating means adding phonons, and at low T only the cheap small-k (low-ω) modes are occupied. Phonons are quanta of collective modes, not little particles bouncing between atoms, and the left animation is the classical-amplitude picture of the same state.

What to notice

  • Pick Single mode and step k from small to the zone edge: the wave gets shorter, ω rises as 2|sin(ka/2)|, and at ka = π neighbours move in antiphase (ω = 2ωD, the maximum). Compare with the sound line ω ≈ ωDa|k| at small k.
  • Press a† create repeatedly: each phonon adds ħωk to the energy and the wave amplitude grows as √(nk+½), so the lattice motion is literally the occupation numbers on the right.
  • Slide T up in Thermal mixture: the green band (ħω < kBT) sweeps up the dispersion, phonons pile into low-k modes first, ΔU follows N kBT once kBT ≳ 2ħωD, and at low T it is exponentially suppressed. Snapshots fluctuate; their average sits on the nB curve.
Model and assumptions
  • Monatomic chain, nearest-neighbour harmonic springs, longitudinal motion, N atoms on a ring (periodic BCs), so k = p·2π/(Na) with N allowed values in (−π/a, π/a]. The zone-edge mode ka = π exists only for even N.
  • Units ħ = kB = a = m = κ = ωD = 1 (so kBT is in units of ħωD; here ωD = √(κ/m) is the spring frequency, not the Debye cut-off).
  • The k = 0 mode (ω = 0) is a rigid translation of the ring, not a vibration; it is drawn as a hollow point and omitted from occupations and ΔU. At high T, ΔU therefore approaches (N−1)kBT, which for N ≫ 1 is the equipartition value N kBT.
  • Each mode is a travelling wave un = Ak cos(kna − ωkt + φk), Ak = √(2ħ(nk+½)/(N m ωk)), so that the classical energy equals ħωk(nk+½). Phases φk are random and fixed per resample. This is the classical-amplitude picture; it is exact for the chain's equation of motion and evolved analytically (no time-stepping error).
  • Snapshot occupations are drawn from the Bose–Einstein (geometric) distribution P(n) = (1−e−ħω/kBT) e−nħω/kBT using fixed uniform random numbers, so raising T only adds phonons. Mean occupation nB has no chemical potential because phonon number is not conserved. Manual a† / a edits persist until you resample.
  • Displacements are magnified by G on screen; the readout gives the true rms displacement. Phonons are quanta of the collective modes, not particles travelling between atoms; the dots are a bookkeeping device for nk.
